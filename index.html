SpecificationThe material

Coir pith is what is left when a coconut husk is separated into fibre.

Compressed, it ships as a dense block at a fraction of its working volume. Wetted, it opens back into an airy, water-holding medium that professional growers use in place of peat.

What varies between one processor and the next is exactly what matters commercially: how thoroughly the pith has been washed, whether it has been buffered, how it has been screened, and how consistent the moisture is at the point of compression. Those are the things we check before a container is loaded, and the things we put in writing before you order.

InspectionWhat we check

Two containers of cocopeat at the same price can be two different products.

Salt content, screening and moisture are where cocopeat goes wrong, and none of them are visible in a photograph. So the work sits before the shipment, not after the complaint: agreeing the grade in writing, checking the material at the processor against it, and holding the batch analysis on file.

Where you need a sample before committing, we will send one from the same production the order will be drawn from.

The facilities photographed on this site are partner processing units we work with in South India. They are not our own plant, and we will gladly arrange a visit.

Certificate of originIndia

Coir comes from where coconuts are already being processed for something else.

Coir pith is a by-product of husk processing, and India's husk processing sits in the south — the coconut belt running through Tamil Nadu, Kerala and coastal Karnataka. That is why the material is available there at scale and at a workable price, and it is where we buy.

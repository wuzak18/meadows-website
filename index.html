01 The material

A by-product that behaves like an engineered substrate.

Coir pith is what is left when a coconut husk is worked for fibre. Nobody grows a coconut for it, which is the whole reason it can be bought from India at a workable price.

Compressed, it ships as a dense block at a fraction of its working volume. Wetted, it opens into a medium that holds water and air at the same time and behaves predictably under a drip line. That is why professional growers use it in place of peat.

Because it is a by-product, it is only as consistent as the process it falls out of. Two units in the same district can turn out material that performs differently. Choosing the unit matters more than choosing the country, and that is the part of this trade we do.

02 Where it goes wrong

Cocopeat goes wrong quietly.

Salt, screening and moisture are the three things that ruin a container, and not one of them is visible in a photograph. A sample can be perfect and the shipment still be wrong, because the sample came from a different week's production.

So the material is not really what you are buying on a first order. What you are buying is a document, agreed before anything is pressed, and someone standing in front of the press who is accountable to it.

04 Compression

A block is a shipping format. The medium only exists once it is wetted.

Compression is what makes cocopeat worth shipping, because you are moving the medium at a fraction of its working volume. Choose a state.

State 01 / as loaded

Dense, dry and stackable

This is the state that makes the freight arithmetic work. It is also the state in which every quality decision has already been taken and can no longer be changed, which is why the specification comes first.

State 02 / water added

Breaking down under water

Add water and the block gives way from the outside in. How cleanly it does that, and how much water it takes, follows directly from the compression ratio and the moisture at the press.

State 03 / ready to fill

Open, airy, holding water

This is what your grower is actually buying. The expansion figure for your batch comes off its analysis rather than off this page, because it moves with the compression ratio and the moisture at pressing.

06 What sits behind the supply

Presses, a sealed container, and a plant that is not ours.

We do not own a processing plant, and we do not claim to

The presses and yards above belong to partner processing units in South India that we buy from and work alongside. None of them are ours, and we will arrange a visit to any of them. What is ours is the part that decides whether your container is right: choosing the unit that can actually make what you asked for, writing the specification down, checking the material against it, and carrying the responsibility for what arrives at your port. 07 Before you write to us

The questions buyers actually ask.

What is your minimum?

A container is the usual first order. Smaller trial quantities can sometimes be arranged where it makes commercial sense. Tell us the volume you have in mind and we will be straight with you about it.

Can I see a sample first?

Yes, and it will be drawn from the production your order would come from rather than off a shelf. That is the only kind of sample worth judging a shipment by.

How long does it take?

It depends on the format, the quantity, and whether the material needs washing or buffering. Once the specification is settled you get a date rather than a range. Lead times stretch in the wet months, and we will say so rather than discover it later.

What if it does not match on arrival?

The agreed specification and the batch analysis are the reference, which is exactly why both exist before production. Send photographs and the analysis and we deal with it against that document, not against a memory of a conversation.

Do you supply washed, buffered or low-salt material?

All three are specified per order, to the crop. Buffering in particular is named to the crop rather than sold as a grade, because that is the only way it means anything.

Can you do private label?

Yes. Sleeves, cartons and shipping marks are printed to your artwork and your specification. Send the artwork with your enquiry and it gets priced in from the start.

Which Incoterms do you work on?

FOB, CFR or CIF. Tell us how you normally buy and we will quote on your terms rather than ours.

Why buy from a sourcing business rather than a factory?

Because no single unit is best at everything. Washed block, buffered slab and screened loose pith are not necessarily strongest at the same address, and a buyer tied to one plant gets whatever that plant happens to be good at. We are not tied.
